Alongside piloting Run and Build at Disneyland Paris, a large part of my work was making sure the backlog itself stayed usable as a coordination tool across four teams with different vocabularies and priorities.

## Context

Product, Design, IT, and QA each worked from their own understanding of the platform:

- each team had its own definition of "done" and its own priorities
- business requests arrived in non-technical language IT couldn't estimate directly
- QA was often looped in only after a story was already built

## Problem

Without a shared, well-groomed backlog, this setup made it hard to:

- **avoid duplicated or gap-owned work** across teams
- **estimate and plan** requests written in business language
- **catch testability issues** before a story reached a sprint
- **trust the backlog** as an up-to-date planning source

## My Role

Functional bridge between business needs and technical delivery: writing and grooming User Stories, running cadrage (framing) sessions, and preparing stories for QA and IT before they entered a sprint.

## Problems Encountered

- Requests arrived from business stakeholders in non-technical language that IT couldn't estimate or plan against directly.
- QA was sometimes brought in too late, after a story was already built, leading to late-stage rework.
- Without consistent grooming, the backlog accumulated stale or duplicate items that slowed down planning sessions.

## Solutions Built

**Framing.** Ran cadrage workshops to convert business requests into functional specifications and well-formed User Stories with clear acceptance criteria.

**QA integration.** Brought QA into story refinement earlier, so testability was considered before a story was committed to a sprint.

**Backlog hygiene.** Established a regular grooming cadence to keep the backlog current and trusted as the single source of planning truth.

**Cross-team resolution.** Coordinated directly with Product and Design to resolve ambiguity before it reached IT, reducing the number of stories bounced back mid-sprint.

## Business Value Delivered

- **Faster, more predictable sprint planning** — fewer stories bounced back for missing information.
- **Less late-stage rework** — testability considered during refinement instead of after build.
- **A trustworthy backlog** — current and free of stale or duplicate items.
- **Fewer cross-team escalations** — ambiguity resolved before reaching IT.

## Technical Stack & Why Each Tool Was Selected

- **JIRA** — the backlog and sprint-tracking tool, chosen because it was already the shared system of record across Product, Design, IT, and QA — no new tool to onboard people onto.
- **Agile/Scrum** — the delivery framework, selected because its recurring ceremonies (refinement, planning, review) gave a natural point to catch ambiguity and misalignment before stories were built rather than after.
