Alongside piloting Run and Build, a large part of my work at Disneyland Paris was making sure the backlog itself stayed usable as a coordination tool across four different teams with different vocabularies and priorities.

## Context

Product, Design, IT, and QA each worked from their own understanding of what "done" meant and what mattered most. Without a shared, well-groomed backlog, work either duplicated across teams or fell into gaps nobody owned.

## My Role

I acted as the functional bridge between business needs and technical delivery: writing and grooming User Stories, running the framing (cadrage) sessions that turned vague requests into buildable specs, and making sure QA and IT had what they needed before a story entered a sprint.

## Problems Encountered

- Requests arrived from business stakeholders in non-technical language that IT couldn't estimate or plan against directly.
- QA was sometimes brought in too late, after a story was already built, leading to late-stage rework.
- Without consistent grooming, the backlog accumulated stale or duplicate items that made planning sessions slower and less trustworthy.

## Solutions Built

- Ran cadrage (framing) workshops to convert business requests into functional specifications and well-formed User Stories with clear acceptance criteria.
- Brought QA into story refinement earlier, so testability was considered before a story was committed to a sprint.
- Established a regular backlog-grooming cadence to keep the backlog current and trusted as the single source of planning truth.
- Coordinated directly with Product and Design to resolve ambiguity before it reached IT, reducing the number of stories bounced back mid-sprint.

## Results

Sprint planning became faster and more predictable, with fewer stories bounced back for missing information, and QA involvement earlier in the cycle reduced late-stage rework.

## Technical Stack & Why Each Tool Was Selected

- **JIRA** — the backlog and sprint-tracking tool, chosen because it was already the shared system of record across Product, Design, IT, and QA — no new tool to onboard people onto.
- **Agile/Scrum** — the delivery framework, selected because its recurring ceremonies (refinement, planning, review) gave a natural point to catch ambiguity and misalignment before stories were built rather than after.
