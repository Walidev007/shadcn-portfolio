This was the founder's first project after leaving a sales manager position at Hermès France to start his own company; he had the client relationships and market knowledge but no operational system yet, and no in-house technical team.

## Context

The client is a company specialized in **organizing physical sales operations for brands**, including:

- staff sales
- public sales
- *friends & family* operations
- charity sales

These operations involve several key steps today:

- collecting the information needed to organize the sale
- building a quote
- adjusting it through addenda
- tracking commercial performance
- final invoicing

These processes were **fragmented across different tools and manual handling**, which caused:

- a lack of centralized information
- a risk of errors when modifying quotes
- limited traceability of versions
- difficulty analyzing the performance of each operation

## Problem

The existing way of working made it hard to:

- **structure incoming sales-operation requests**
- **generate quotes quickly and reliably**
- **manage modifications and addenda**
- **centralize operational data**
- **analyze commercial performance**

These limitations slowed down operation preparation and made it harder to follow the business as a whole.

## My Role

Product owner and no-code product builder, full scope: functional design, cost/subscription estimation for the client, data modeling, workflow automation, frontend build, and client-facing delivery and onboarding. I was the only person building this, end to end.

## Problems Encountered

- The client couldn't visualize an abstract system from a spec document alone — he needed to *see* something before he could give useful feedback.
- Softr (the frontend layer) couldn't natively render some of the specific charts the client needed — e.g. sales quantity by seller and by hour interval, broken down by category — a hard tool limitation, not a data problem.
- The document-generation tool (DocsAutomator) required a very specific JSON payload structure, and its own documentation was incomplete and inconsistent with what the tool actually expected — repeated trial and error, plus a support ticket, was needed to get the payload structure right.
- The client wanted branded invoices and quotes but had no existing templates of his own — only examples from his previous employer, which had to be redesigned from scratch as reusable templates with dynamic placeholders and conditional sections.
- Review cycles were slow: the client needed time to check every delivery (database structure, Softr screens, seeded product/sales data) before validating the next step, so the whole build had to be sequenced and communicated carefully rather than delivered as one big drop.

## Solutions Built

**Step 1 — Audit & framing.** Acted as product owner for this phase: audited the client's existing manual process piece by piece, logged every requirement and decision in a dedicated Notion workspace, defined the MVP scope, planned every build step, estimated the full cost upfront (my service fee plus the tool subscriptions the client would need to pay for), and produced functional designs — schemas and flow diagrams — before any development started.

**Step 2 — Development & implementation.** Built the Airtable base: tables, relational links, and scripts, with solid documentation, evolving it with small incremental changes as the client's requirements were refined during the build rather than re-architecting from scratch each time. In parallel, gathered and set up the accounts and logins needed across the automation toolchain (Softr, Make, DocsAutomator) so the integrations could go live without credential gaps mid-build.

**Difficulties and how they were resolved:**

- The client couldn't visualize an abstract system from a spec alone — he had no mental picture of what was being built. I spent one day building a throwaway frontend MVP specifically so he could *see* the direction early. Once we'd discussed the details and answered his questions against that prototype, I integrated the validated frontend properly using Softr's real components and functions.
- Some elements didn't behave as expected in the MVP frontend — specifically, certain charts (sales quantity by seller and by hour interval, broken down by category) couldn't be built directly in Softr due to a hard tool limitation. Rather than compromise the workflow the client had already validated, I built custom scripts that fetched and shaped the exact data he needed by date interval and data type, as a workaround.
- Review cycles were slow by nature of the client's process: he needed time to check every delivery — the database structure, the Softr frontend, the seeded product/sales data. I planned the whole build around this and kept a continuous discussion going so each part was validated in sequence rather than delivered as one big drop.
- The client wanted branded invoices and quotes but didn't have templates for the new company — only examples from his previous role. I took those examples and turned them into reusable templates. The document-generation tool then turned out to have a payload structure requirement (unlike a simpler tool such as Google Docs) that its own documentation described incompletely. I had to make sure the JSON payload sent from the Make node matched the tool's expected object structure, contacted the vendor's support, and used Claude Code to help write the script that transformed my payload into the correct structure. I mapped the data inside the Make workflow node, built dedicated routes per document type, tested them, and verified output against the tool's own logs.
- Documented everything as it was solved: the payload structure, template placeholders, conditional show/hide sections, and calculation formulas — so the system stays maintainable without me.

**Delivery.** Linked the document-generation tool into Softr buttons, wiring each one to trigger the corresponding Make workflow. Took the time to live-test the full system and present it to the client page by page, feature by feature, before final validation. The client validated the tool and paid the final part of the cost.

## Business Value Delivered

The client went from a fully manual process (Airtable forms + email) to a fully integrated no-code system:

- **A real database** — replacing scattered Airtable forms and email threads with a single structured source of truth.
- **A modern, tablet- and mobile-friendly frontend** — usable on the floor during operations, not just at a desk.
- **Invoice and quote generation from his own templates** — branded documents produced automatically instead of built by hand.
- **User and brand management** — every brand and user accounted for in one system instead of tracked informally.
- **Sales briefs with full versioning** — every detail and change to a brief tracked and historized.
- **A delivered, adopted MVP** — the client was onboarded and can now use the system and make changes himself, in both Softr and Airtable.

- **Full centralization of operations** — every brief, quote, addendum, and invoice lives in one system instead of scattered forms and email threads.
- **Reduced sales-preparation time** — briefs, quotes, and addenda are generated and updated in minutes instead of manual back-and-forth.
- **Complete document traceability** — quotes, addenda, and invoices are all versioned and historized.
- **Better commercial performance analysis** — operational KPIs (sales by category/hour/seller, no-show rate, commission bonuses) are tracked directly in the system instead of reconstructed after the fact.
- **A scalable model for future growth** — the data and automation architecture is built to extend as the business adds new operation types or volume.

## Technical Stack & Why Each Tool Was Selected

- **Airtable** — chosen as the data and business-logic layer because it gives a brand-new one-person company a real relational database with a client-editable interface, without the cost or delay of a custom backend.
- **Softr** — selected for the frontend because it builds directly on top of Airtable and produces a tablet/mobile-friendly interface fast, which mattered given the tight MVP budget and timeline.
- **Make** — the automation and integration layer connecting Airtable, Softr, and the document-generation tool; chosen over more code-heavy alternatives because it kept the whole system maintainable by a non-developer client after handover.
- **DocsAutomator** — used for automated PDF quote/invoice generation from the client's own templates; despite the documentation friction, it was the right tool once the payload structure was solved, since it let the client keep his real, branded document designs rather than generic auto-formatted output.
- **Claude Code** — used to help reverse-engineer and script the correct JSON payload transformation for DocsAutomator, turning a documentation gap into a solved integration faster than manual trial and error alone.
- **Notion** — used as the single source of truth for requirements, decisions, and scope throughout the audit and build, so nothing discussed with the client got lost between sessions.
