Building an automation is only half the job — if the team that owns the process can't understand or adjust it, it breaks the first time something changes. This is the training and change-management side of my work at WIDO.

## Context

Clients receiving a new AI agent or automated workflow were often automation-literate in theory but had never operated one in practice. Without training, they'd either be afraid to touch the automation (freezing it in its initial state) or would tweak it in ways that broke the logic.

## My Role

I design and deliver the training that follows every automation project I build: session content, hands-on exercises using the client's actual workflow, and the documentation the team keeps afterward. I act as trainer, not just builder.

## Problems Encountered

- Non-technical teams needed to understand automation concepts (triggers, conditions, data mapping) without a technical background.
- Generic training material didn't stick — people needed to practice on their own real workflow, not a toy example.
- Some teams were wary of AI agents making decisions on their behalf, which needed to be addressed directly rather than glossed over.

## Solutions Built

- Built training sessions structured around the client's actual deployed automation rather than abstract examples, so trainees immediately saw how concepts applied to their own process.
- Created step-by-step documentation covering how the workflow behaves, how to make safe changes, and how to recognize when something needs escalation back to me.
- Ran live walkthroughs where team members made a supervised change to the automation themselves during the session, to build confidence before going solo.
- Addressed AI-trust concerns explicitly by walking through what the agent does and doesn't decide, and where a human checkpoint remains in the loop.

## Results

Teams left able to operate their automation independently, with documented processes to fall back on — reducing the amount of ongoing support needed after each project and increasing adoption of the tools instead of them being abandoned out of distrust.

## Technical Stack & Why Each Tool Was Selected

- **n8n / Make** — training built directly on the platform used in production, since the goal was operational competence on the real tool, not a generic concept.
- **ChatGPT / Claude** — used in the training itself to show trainees how to safely prompt and validate the LLM steps inside their automation.
- **Written documentation (process runbooks)** — chosen over video-only training because teams needed something searchable and referenceable weeks after the session, when the actual question came up.
