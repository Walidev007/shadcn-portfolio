Building an automation is only half the job at WIDO — if the team that owns the process can't understand or adjust it, it breaks the first time something changes. This is the training and change-management side of my work.

## Context

Clients receiving a new AI agent or automated workflow were often willing but had never operated one in practice:

- they understood the value of automation in theory, but not the mechanics
- they had no documentation to fall back on once I moved to the next project
- some were wary of an AI agent making decisions on their behalf

## Problem

Without training, client teams ran into two failure modes:

- **freezing the automation** — too afraid to touch it, so it stayed stuck in its initial state as the business evolved
- **breaking the automation** — tweaking it without understanding the underlying logic, and introducing errors

Both outcomes meant the automation stopped delivering value shortly after I moved on.

## My Role

Trainer and change-management lead for every automation project I build: designing session content, running hands-on exercises on the client's real workflow, and producing the documentation the team keeps afterward.

## Problems Encountered

- Non-technical teams needed to understand automation concepts (triggers, conditions, data mapping) without a technical background.
- Generic training material didn't stick — people needed to practice on their own real workflow, not a toy example.
- Some teams were wary of AI agents making decisions on their behalf, which needed to be addressed directly rather than glossed over.

## Solutions Built

**Session design.** Built training sessions structured around the client's actual deployed automation rather than abstract examples, so trainees immediately saw how concepts applied to their own process.

**Documentation.** Created step-by-step runbooks covering how the workflow behaves, how to make safe changes, and when to escalate back to me.

**Hands-on practice.** Ran live walkthroughs where team members made a supervised change to the automation themselves during the session, to build confidence before going solo.

**Trust building.** Addressed AI-trust concerns explicitly by walking through what the agent does and doesn't decide, and where a human checkpoint remains in the loop.

## Business Value Delivered

- **Operational independence** — teams left able to run their automation without me.
- **Reduced ongoing support load** — fewer support requests after each project thanks to documented runbooks.
- **Higher adoption** — automations kept in active use instead of being abandoned out of distrust or confusion.

## Technical Stack & Why Each Tool Was Selected

- **n8n / Make** — training built directly on the platform used in production, since the goal was operational competence on the real tool, not a generic concept.
- **ChatGPT / Claude** — used in the training itself to show trainees how to safely prompt and validate the LLM steps inside their automation.
- **Written documentation (process runbooks)** — chosen over video-only training because teams needed something searchable and referenceable weeks after the session, when the actual question came up.
